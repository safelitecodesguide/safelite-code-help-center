Codes and insurance: the honest version

This is the question that wastes the most quotes. The short version: if insurance is paying, promo codes are off the table. If you are paying yourself, they are very much on it. Here is the full picture, including the one official group discount.

Why codes fail on insurance claims

Safelite's offer terms state the discounts are valid when scheduling on safelite.com and are not valid on insurance claims (or on commercial/fleet services). The booking flow still shows an “I have a promo code” link on the insurance path — that is a quirk of the flow, not an exception to the terms. In checkout testing, the codes that work are the ones applied on the pay-on-my-own path.

So when do codes save you money?

  • Paying out of pocket: all 14 confirmed codes apply — up to $100 off a replacement with ADDS100.
  • Comparing routes: if your deductible is close to the out-of-pocket quote minus $100, paying yourself with a code can be the cheaper route. Run both numbers before deciding — the quotes on the costs page show the scale.
  • Insurance paying in full: there is no code to add; your cost is set by your policy, not by a promo.

The group discount: 10THANKYOU10

Safelite's official group offer — for military members, AAA and AARP members, and first responders — runs through the code 10THANKYOU10. What testing and Safelite's own terms establish:

  • It is percentage-based on a subtotal, not a clean 10% of the final total: on the $787.63 sample replacement quote it took off $39.08 (about 5%).
  • Eligibility is verified at the appointment — bring the relevant membership or service proof.
  • It excludes recalibration, insurance claims and deductibles, per the offer terms.
  • It does not stack with another code — for most replacement quotes ADDS100's flat $100 is worth more, so compare both before booking.
  • There is no separate AAA code that works: AAA10SL was rejected at checkout. AAA members use 10THANKYOU10.
Not sure which route is cheaper? Get the pay-on-my-own quote, apply ADDS100, and compare that total with your deductible. That one comparison — your real numbers, not averages — settles it.
